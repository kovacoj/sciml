### Weak formulation on the whole line

The harmonic oscillator is posed on $\Omega=\mathbb{R}$ with unbounded potential $x^2$. The natural energy space is the weighted Sobolev space

$$
V := \bigl\{ u \in H^1(\mathbb{R}) : xu \in L^2(\mathbb{R}) \bigr\}
     = H^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap L^2(\mathbb{R}; x^2\,\d{x}).
$$

For $u,v \in V$ the bilinear form

$$
a(u,v)
:=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} u'v'\,\d{x}
+
\int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2 uv\,\d{x}
$$

is well defined because Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2 uv\,\d{x}\right|
=
\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}} (xu)(xv)\,\d{x}\right|
\leq
|xu|_{L^2}\,|xv|_{L^2}
< \infty.
$$

The norm on $V$ is

$$
|u|_V^2
:=
|u|_{L^2}^2 + |u'|_{L^2}^2 + |xu|_{L^2}^2.
$$

In one dimension the $L^2$ term is controlled by the other two. For $u \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$,

$$
0
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \bigl(x|u|^2\bigr)'\,\d{x}
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} |u|^2\,\d{x}
+
2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}} xu'\,\overline{u}\,\d{x},
$$

hence

$$
|u|_{L^2}^2
\leq
2|u'|_{L^2}\,|xu|_{L^2}
\leq
|u'|_{L^2}^2 + |xu|_{L^2}^2.
$$

By density this holds for all $u \in V$. Consequently

$$
|u|_V^2
\leq
2\bigl(|u'|_{L^2}^2 + |xu|_{L^2}^2\bigr),
$$

so the seminorm $|u|_a^2 := |u'|_{L^2}^2 + |xu|_{L^2}^2$ is equivalent to $|u|_V^2$.

### Lax–Milgram verification

We seek $u \in V$ such that $a(u,v) = \ell(v)$ for all $v \in V$, where $\ell(v) = 2\langle f,v\rangle_{V^*,V}$ and $f \in V^*$.

1. **$V$ is a Hilbert space.**  
   If $(u_n)$ is Cauchy in $V$, then $u_n \to u$ in $H^1(\mathbb{R})$ and $xu_n \to g$ in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$. Local convergence implies $g=xu$, so $xu \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and $u \in V$.

2. **Continuity of $a$.**  
   $$
   |a(u,v)|
   \leq
   |u'|_{L^2}|v'|_{L^2} + |xu|_{L^2}|xv|_{L^2}
   \leq
   |u|_V\,|v|_V.
   $$

3. **Coercivity of $a$.**  
   $$
   a(u,u)
   =
   |u'|_{L^2}^2 + |xu|_{L^2}^2
   \geq
   \tfrac12 |u|_V^2.
   $$
   The coercivity constant is $\alpha = \tfrac12$.

4. **Continuity of $\ell$.**  
   $$
   |\ell(v)|
   =
   2|\langle f,v\rangle|
   \leq
   2|f|_{V^*}\,|v|_V.
   $$

All Lax–Milgram assumptions hold, so there exists a unique $u \in V$ with the a priori bound $|u|_V \leq 2|f|_{V^*}$.

### Uniqueness argument

If $u_1,u_2 \in V$ are solutions, let $w=u_1-u_2$. Subtracting the weak equations and testing with $v=w$ gives

$$
\int_{\mathbb{R}} |w'|^2\,\d{x}
+
\int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2|w|^2\,\d{x}
= 0.
$$

Both integrals are nonnegative, so $w'=0$ a.e. and $xw=0$ a.e. Thus $w$ is constant and $w=0$ a.e. (the only constant in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$).

### Bounded vs. unbounded domain

On a bounded interval $\Omega=(-L,L)$ the potential $x^2$ is bounded, so $V=H^1(\Omega)$ or $H_0^1(\Omega)$ suffices. On $\mathbb{R}$ the weighted space is essential: $u(x)=(1+x^2)^{-1/2}$ belongs to $H^1(\mathbb{R})$ but $\int_{\mathbb{R}} x^2 u(x)^2\,\d{x} = \infty$.

### Boundary terms in integration by parts

When deriving the weak form, start with test functions $v \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ so that boundary terms vanish due to compact support. Extend to all $v \in V$ by density. Do not claim pointwise vanishing at infinity for arbitrary $H^1$ functions; while $u(x) \to 0$ as $|x| \to \infty$ for $u \in H^1(\mathbb{R})$, the derivative $u'(x)$ need not tend to zero.
